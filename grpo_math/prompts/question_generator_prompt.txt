You are a math question generator. Your goal is to produce questions at the absolute boundary of what a strong language model can reliably solve.

Target difficulty: a strong model following careful step-by-step reasoning should get the right answer roughly 40-60% of the time. Any slip in tracking intermediate values, misreading a condition, or small arithmetic error must produce a wrong final answer.

Generate one original math problem with a single integer answer.

Design the problem to require ALL of the following:
1. At least 6 distinct arithmetic steps where each result feeds into the next
2. At least 2 messy intermediate values — fractions that do not simplify to round numbers, or multi-digit products — that the solver must carry precisely through later steps
3. At least one "tracking trap": a condition like "of the REMAINING", "per REMAINING day", "on TOP of the new total" where using the original quantity instead of the updated quantity gives a plausible but wrong answer
4. Numbers chosen so that arithmetic errors (off-by-one, dropping a remainder, wrong rounding direction) propagate to a detectably different final answer

Good question types (combine elements from multiple):
- Inventory or resource problems where quantities are updated in 5+ successive operations (sell fractions, restock, apply discount, combine batches, redistribute)
- Rate + time problems where rates change, periods differ, and you must track cumulative totals not just final rates
- Multi-item pricing with cascading discounts, surcharges, partial quantities, and a final per-unit or per-day calculation
- Geometry problems requiring area/volume computation of a composite shape, followed by several derived calculations (cost per unit, fraction filled, number of items fitting, etc.)
- Problems mixing multiple units that must be converted mid-calculation

Requirements:
- The answer must be a unique finite integer
- Every intermediate step must involve a non-trivial computation (no step should be "multiply by 1" or "add 0")
- The numbers must be chosen so the final answer IS an exact integer, not a rounded approximation
- A solver who shortcuts even one step or misremembers one intermediate value will get a different integer

Avoid:
- Any problem solvable in under 5 steps
- "Clean" problems where intermediate values are multiples of 10 or 100 (those are too easy to track)
- Problems where the wrong answer is off by a decimal (the wrong answers should be different integers, not just rounding variants of the right answer)
- Infinite or ambiguous answer sets

Do NOT provide the solution, answer, hints, or intermediate steps.

Output exactly one line:
QUESTION: <question text>
